# PACK: HEALTH (semi-realistic panels)

Health, food and drink, herbs and spices, vitamins and minerals, organs and body systems, symptoms, medicine and wellness explainers. This pack **defines the visual style of the whole skill** (the STYLE line of `template.md`); the Finance pack only swaps the heroes. Applies on top of `rules.md`, `layouts.md` and `template.md`.

THE STARS OF EVERY CANVAS ARE FOOD, DRINKS, NUTRIENTS, ORGANS AND THE BODY. People are supporting actors and appear only when a cue needs them.

## HERO RULE (every panel)
The hero is the thing the cue is about, decided from its main noun and meaning, in this order:
1. Food, drink, herb, spice, supplement, ingredient: the food or drink whole and large, at least half of the panel, in front and sharp, on one simple surface (wooden board, stone slab, ceramic plate, glass, steel tray) with at most one supporting prop. Preparation shows cropped hands only (no face, the same skin tone every time) or the finished item.
2. Organ, gland, body system, internal structure: the organ or a clean cross-section is the whole picture, large on a deep dark backdrop, anatomically accurate, fine crosshatch tissue, true size and position, never mixed with a room, bed or person.
3. A place in the body where something happens, or a food's link to a body part: a faint, translucent, faceless torso silhouette (front or side view, named) with the one organ glowing inside at its true position, deep dark backdrop.
4. Vitamin, mineral, molecule, element: that substance (see section "Substances").
5. Pill or drug: generic unmarked tablets, capsules, a strip, a bottle or an inhaler as the main subject; never needles, syringes, overdoses or spilled piles; effect only by association, never curing, cleaning or repairing.
6. A human habit, symptom, feeling, night or sleep scene, family or group situation, patient, or a professional speaking: the person is the hero, and the panel still shows one hero object or body cue from the cue at a third of the frame (the food, a pill bottle, a hand on the affected body part, a 3D organ model on a desk, a glass of the drink).
The hero fills at least half the panel (a third in person panels), sits in front, is the sharpest element and is never hidden or outsized by a person. A cue about a food, organ or nutrient gets no person. Never add a person to make the canvas feel like a film.
- A cue linking a food or organ to a human feeling is ONE panel: choose the hero by the cue's main noun; draw both together only when the person is eating, holding or preparing the item right then, with the food large in the foreground.
- A comparison cue (or two matching cues side by side) shows two matching drawings in one panel separated by a natural element, differing only in what is compared.
- Time or process: matching drawings or visible time cues in one panel; never arrows inside a panel.

## ORGAN AND BODY RULES
1. One clear view (front, side or clean cross-section), named in the panel text. 2. LEFT AND RIGHT written in words for every organ panel: in a front view the viewer's left is the person's right (for example "the liver sits on the viewer's left, the heart tilts toward the viewer's right"); say which way a paired organ's curved side faces. 3. Only the structures the cue needs; faint unlabeled landmarks only when position matters. 4. The same organ keeps the same view, proportions, color and body side across the file. 5. Healthy versus affected on matching drawings changes only size, thickness, tone, glow or surface texture, never holes, wounds, blood or rot. 6. An effect goes where the script puts it; never invent a location or result. 7. A visible outer body part (belly, knee, skin, hands) is a person panel showing the part through pose; its inside is a different cue. 8. Organs are clean, accurate, stylized anatomy, detailed but never graphic: no blood, wounds, surgery, needles, open bodies or dead tissue. **No arrowhead label lines inside panels** on a canvas: parts the cue names are named by the panel's title label or caption box.

## SUBSTANCES (vitamin, mineral, element, molecule)
- Vitamin: its letter exactly as the script writes it (C, D3, B12) on a large round tag next to the sources the cue names, each whole and recognisable in one tidy row (up to 4). Several vitamins: one same-size tag each in a straight row. Never imply a cure.
- Molecule: a clean glossy ball-and-stick model, correct atom counts and bonds, formula exactly as in the script; complex molecules (glucose, cholesterol, amino acids, proteins) as a simplified clean ring or chain skeleton, never an invented structure. One fixed color per element for the whole video (carbon charcoal-brown, oxygen bright red, hydrogen pale warm white, nitrogen bright blue, sodium amber yellow, chlorine bright green). A molecule is shown alone.
- Element tile: the big symbol in correct case with the element name in capitals; atomic number only if the cue states it; named source foods beside it.
- These sit on a calm deep dark near-black navy backdrop in the panel (a plain panel background, no room or scenery).

## TEXT INSIDE PANELS (a short list of exceptions; otherwise none)
Panels carry no text. Exceptions, each listed in the panel description with its carrier: (1) a vitamin letter on its tag, an element symbol on its tile, a formula or atom symbols on a molecule model; (2) one numeral the cue states as the point of the panel (a dose, a duration, a count) as plain numerals on a natural carrier (a round tile, a plain price or measuring tag), never in a person, interior or food scene unless the cue is that number; (3) the fixed words "NOT MEDICAL ADVICE" on a notebook cover, "LIKE" and "SUBSCRIBE" on a sign. Letters, symbols and numerals use bold hand-lettering, one size per panel, in a calm patch of backdrop, and are spelled exactly as the script writes them. Each quantity appears once.

## PEOPLE
- **MAIN MAN** (fixed main character; the user attaches his reference image in the image tool): mid-30s, short messy dark hair, round friendly face, medium height, broad shoulders, lean athletic build; skin tone, eyes, eyebrows and any distinguishing feature come from the attached image. Never write out his face, hair or build. `CHARACTER RULES` line for him: "The main man, matching the attached reference image exactly (same face, hair and build). His clothing in this scene is: (the full outfit for this scene)." Default outfit when the scene gives no other cue: bright sky-blue button shirt with rolled sleeves and one chest pocket, tan trousers, brown lace-up shoes. Outfit follows place, time and role (pajamas in bed, apron in the kitchen), written in full and kept word for word within a canvas. Shirtless only when the cue needs the torso (belly, chest, back, skin, breathing), always with shorts or trousers, in a neutral educational pose. He is the viewer's stand-in and the narrator of "I" and "you" scripts; he is a doctor or professional ONLY when the script says so.
- **OTHER CHARACTERS** (words only): created only when a cue mentions or clearly implies that person (doctor, nurse, dietitian, pharmacist, parent, child, partner, friend). One short locked line each: gender and age group, hair in a few words, clothing with colors that shows the role, role props, one clear feature only if it helps (glasses, grey hair). No skin shade, face shape or build. Clearly different from the main man and every other character. A doctor is always a new character (coat, stethoscope), takes no more than a third of the panel and stands with a hero object; never an empty talking head. Groups: a mix of ages and genders in one short sentence, visible faces, a visible hero in the middle. Gender: follow stated gender; if open, rotate female, male, female, male by first appearance. Every character shows a real feeling and an active pose (sitting up in bed, cooking, eating, reading a label).
- **BALANCE:** aim for at least about 60 percent of panels in a batch with no person unless the cues truly need more; no character in two consecutive panels of a canvas; vary places (bed, kitchen, clinic, market, park), distance and frame side.
- **NAMED PEOPLE:** one name = one character for the file; the story's main male person takes the main man; names never appear in pictures.

## SETTING AND ERA
**Script-only setting:** every panel is built from the script's own words. With no place or culture named, the default setting is an ordinary modern American one (home, kitchen, street, clinic, people, clothing); never default to an Indian or any other region; follow a named place or culture exactly. A cue about the past (ancient, long ago, our ancestors, before refrigerators) is drawn entirely in that period: keep each character's face, hair and build, replace modern clothing with simple period clothing (undyed linen or wool, muted earth colors), clay pots, open fires, stone grinding, wooden tools; quantities by counted items, notches or a knotted rope, never a clock or printed calendar.

## INVISIBLE IDEAS
Never draw invisible things as bottles, droplets, clouds or glowing symbols. A hormone, enzyme or neurotransmitter is shown through the gland or organ that makes or receives it. Stress, anxiety, tiredness, pain or worry is a person in a real situation (sitting up in bed at night) through posture, face, hands and props. "Signal" or "same effect": the same drawing or reaction repeated across situations. Excess, deficiency, damage or improvement: visible differences in size, tone, glow, posture or detail on the same drawing or person. Slow damage over time: matching before-and-after drawings or visible time cues.

## SPECIAL CUES
- **Disclaimer:** its own one-panel canvas with the ribbon `DISCLAIMER` (see `procedure.md`, Disclaimer canvas). The panel: a new doctor character holding up a small notebook whose cover says "NOT MEDICAL ADVICE", a hero object on the desk beside the doctor. Panel title label "A QUICK NOTE" (never "NOT MEDICAL ADVICE", which is already on the notebook, and never "DISCLAIMER", which is the ribbon).
- **Like and subscribe:** the main man smiling beside a big thumbs-up sign and a bell, with "LIKE" and "SUBSCRIBE" written on the signs. Panel title label "THANKS FOR WATCHING" (never "LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE", which is already on the signs).
- **Recap:** the best-suited character at a table with a row of small sticky notes on the wall, each with one numeral or keyword the cues name. Label "RECAP".
- **Closing question to the viewer:** one calm image that fits the lesson (a kitchen, a quiet park), no text.
- **Caution cue:** a doctor or pharmacist holding a small card that says "CAUTION" beside the relevant item, the item large in the foreground.

## SAFE IMAGERY AND CLAIMS
Never show a food, drink, vitamin or drug curing, detoxing, melting, cleaning or repairing anything. No medical claim, cure or result the cue does not state. No drug brand or dosage unless the cue names it. No decorative filler or stray extra organs. No boards, whiteboards or easels.

## PACK SELF-CHECK
One clear hero per panel at the right size; no person where the cue is about a food, organ or nutrient; organ left and right stated in words; no arrowhead label lines; text only from the exceptions list; the main man's line is exact; other characters differ from him; the American default holds; no cure or claim added; blood and gore absent.
