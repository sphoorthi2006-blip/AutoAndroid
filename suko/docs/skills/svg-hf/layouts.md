# LAYOUTS - Health and Finance (two layout systems, optional ribbon)

Every canvas is made of three **independent choices**:
1. **Heading:** a ribbon or a plain frame (decided by the canvas plan, never by you).
2. **Layout system:** Layout 1 (panel layouts) or Layout 2 (comic layouts).
3. **Layout inside that system:** from the story shape and the panel count.

## 1. THE TWO SYSTEMS
- **Layout 1 - panel layout:** a big rectangular frame holding **circles and rectangles in rows**, with dashed arrows only where one panel directly causes or leads to the next. Circles carry the key moments and main ideas; rectangles carry the supporting beats. Best for explaining an idea, comparing two things, listing parallel items and cause-and-effect chains, where the beats carry roughly equal weight.
- **Layout 2 - comic layout:** the same frame holding **rectangles of different sizes**, the key beat in the biggest panel, no arrows, flow carried by order, size and caption boxes. Best for telling what happened, a story or case with a dominant beat, a turning point, or a sequence where pacing matters.
- The ribbon belongs to neither system: if the canvas plan gives a ribbon text, the ribbon sits centered on the top edge of the frame (frame line broken behind it) and the panels start below it, whichever system is used.

## 2. HOW TO CHOOSE (silently, for every canvas)
1. **Name the job of the paragraph from its first cue:** an opening claim, question or definition = it *explains* (Layout 1). An opening event, scene, person's story or case = it *narrates* (Layout 2). A paragraph that does both is decided by its first cue.
2. **Name the story shape:** hook then details, build-up, turning point, list of parallel items, journey or time passing, contrast or comparison, cause then effect, action or conflict, quiet or reflective moment, one big idea.
3. **Count the beats** = the panel count from the canvas plan. The layout MUST have exactly that many panels. Never pad, never trim.
4. **Find the key beat** and give it the biggest panel (Layout 2) or the circle (Layout 1). If no beat is stronger than the rest, use equal panels.
5. **Set the mood:** calm or explanatory = standard or wide gutter, straight cuts. Fast, tense or shocking (an emergency, a sudden reversal) = tight gutter, and a diagonal layout only for a truly action-like beat; this is rare in health and finance.
6. **Check rhythm** against the layout log in the locks and the prompts already written:
   - never the same layout twice in a row;
   - never more than two canvases in a row from the same system, and a long run of one system gets a break from the other;
   - Health and Finance lean on Layout 1 (they mostly explain) but use Layout 2 for story-like paragraphs: aim for Layout 2 on at least about one canvas in four when the script has any story, case or example;
   - diagonals no more than about one canvas in three of the Layout 2 canvases, grids about one canvas in eight at most;
   - after a busy 5 to 8 panel canvas, a single-panel canvas is a welcome pause.
7. **A single-cue paragraph is a pause or a punch:** Single circle (Layout 1) for a key idea, definition or question; Single splash (Layout 2) for an event or a scene. A short closing line gets a wide gutter and one simple, clear hero.
8. Parts of one split paragraph are separate canvases: same mood, different layouts. The ribbon repeats on every canvas of its section.

## 3. LAYOUT 1 LIBRARY (rows of circles and rectangles)
Rows are horizontal bands; every panel in a row has the same top and bottom edge (a circle's diameter equals its row height); rows never overlap; rows may hold different numbers of panels and are centered. All panels of the same shape and role are the same size (a deliberately larger "feature" or "wide closer" panel is named as such). Even gutters; no panel touches, overlaps or crosses another panel or the frame; every row leaves room for the labels attached under its panels. Story is read left to right in a row, rows top to bottom; PANEL CONTENTS order is exactly this order. No right-to-left rows, no rings, no columns that break the row bands.

| Panels | Layout | Shape roles | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single circle | one large centered circle | key idea, definition, question, closing line |
| 2 to 4 | Chain row | one row of equal circles | a short cause-and-effect chain or ordered steps |
| 3 to 8 | Circles over rectangles | row 1: 1 to 4 circles (key ideas); row 2: 2 to 5 rectangles (supports) | main ideas with their supporting details |
| 3 to 6 | Feature over row | row 1: one large circle (the claim); row 2: 2 to 5 rectangles (the evidence) | one claim and its proofs |
| 4 or 5 | Circles then wide closer | row 1: 3 or 4 equal circles; row 2: one wide rectangle about two thirds of the row width, the largest panel (the outcome) | a chain that ends in a result |
| 4 to 8 | Two sides | row 1: two large circles (the two sides); row 2: 2 to 6 rectangles (details, left side first) | comparison, myth versus fact, right versus avoid |
| 3 or 6 | Staircase rows | 3 panels = rows of 1 then 2; 6 panels = rows of 1, then 2, then 3; the first panel of each row is a circle, the rest rectangles | growth, build-up, decline |
| 4 to 8 | Mixed rows | 2 rows, any mix of circles (key ideas) and rectangles (supporting beats) | when beats differ in weight but the paragraph explains |
| 6 to 8 | Three rows | rows of 2 / 3 / 3 or 3 / 3 / 2 | long lists of parallel items, used sparingly |

**Geometry phrase for the LAYOUT line (Layout 1):** `Rows (comic grid) with {n} row(s){ below the ribbon}. Row 1 holds {k} {shape}(s) left to right, centered: panel 1, panel 2, ... Row 2 holds ...` then the rules bullets of section 5.

**Arrows (Layout 1 only, optional):** an arrow appears only where one panel directly leads to or causes the next, and only between neighbours in a row (horizontal) or panels stacked directly above each other (vertical). Each arrow is one short straight dashed black line with one thick arrowhead, running between the facing edges of the two panels. Arrows never curve, never cross a panel, a label or each other. A canvas whose beats are not a chain has no arrows. The gutters between linked panels are wide enough to hold the arrow.

## 4. LAYOUT 2 LIBRARY (comic layouts)
Rectangles only (square corners), in horizontal row bands (the Tall layouts excepted), no arrows. Percentages are shares of the row width, ratios are row heights.

| Panels | Layout | Key beat | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single splash | the only panel | lone line, hook, pause |
| 2 | Pair | the wider panel | before and after, claim and result, myth and fact told as a story |
| 3 | Widescreen strips | the tallest band | journey, passing time |
| 3 | Tall-left / Tall-right | the tall panel (first or last in reading order) | one hero beat with two supports |
| 4 | Splash + strip / Strip + splash | first / last | hook or outcome with its parts; build-up to a payoff |
| 4 or 6 | Stagger | the widest panel | back and forth, cause and result |
| 4 | Four-grid | none | four parallel items (rare) |
| 5 | Turning point | panel 3 | before, turn, after |
| 5 | Sandwich | panel 3 | setup, main moment, aftermath |
| 5 | Tall-left with bottom row | panel 1 | hero beat, supports, closing pair |
| 6 | Crescendo / Reveal-down | last / first | rising tension; big claim then consequences |
| 6 | Six-grid | none | six parallel items (rare) |
| 7 | Centerpiece | panel 4 | build to a middle peak |
| 8 | Hourglass | panels 4 and 5 | long section with a middle peak |

Geometry phrases (fill the gutter setting and name the key panel as "the biggest panel"):
- **Single splash** (1): `1 row. One wide rectangle, panel 1, about 80 percent of the frame width and filling the frame height below the ribbon, centered.`
- **Pair** (2): `1 row. Two rectangles, panel 1 about 60 percent and panel 2 about 40 percent of the row width (flip if the key beat is second).`
- **Widescreen strips** (3): `3 rows, each one full-width rectangle: panel 1 (short), panel 2 (medium), panel 3 (tall, the largest); row heights in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4.`
- **Tall-left** (3): `Left column: one tall rectangle, panel 1, about 40 percent of the width and the full height, the largest panel. Right column (about 60 percent): two stacked rectangles, panel 2 on top and panel 3 below, each half the height, with a gutter between them tall enough to hold panel 2's label.` Tall-right mirrors it with the tall panel last (stack read first). **With bottom row** (5): that block fills the top band (about 62 percent), the bottom band (about 38 percent) holds panels 4 and 5, 50 / 50.
- **Splash + strip** (4): `2 rows. Row 1: one wide rectangle, panel 1, the largest (height ratio 3). Row 2: three equal rectangles, panels 2 to 4 (height ratio 2).` Strip + splash reverses the rows (the last panel is the largest).
- **Stagger** (4 or 6): `2 (or 3) rows of two rectangles. Row 1: panel 1 about 60 percent, panel 2 about 40 percent. Row 2: panel 3 about 40 percent, panel 4 about 60 percent. (Row 3: 60 / 40.) Equal row heights.`
- **Turning point** (5): `3 rows. Row 1: two rectangles 50 / 50. Row 2: one wide rectangle, panel 3, the largest. Row 3: two rectangles 50 / 50. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Sandwich** (5): `3 rows. Row 1: one wide rectangle. Row 2: three rectangles, about 25 / 50 / 25, the middle one the largest. Row 3: one wide rectangle. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Crescendo** (6): `3 rows. Row 1: three equal rectangles. Row 2: two rectangles 50 / 50. Row 3: one wide rectangle, panel 6, the largest. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 4.` **Reveal-down** (6): rows 1 / 2 / 3 panels, the first wide panel the largest, ratio 4 : 3 : 2.
- **Centerpiece** (7): `3 rows: three equal rectangles, one wide rectangle (the largest), three equal rectangles. Ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Hourglass** (8): `3 rows: three equal rectangles, two wide rectangles (the largest), three equal rectangles. Ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Four-grid** (4) / **Six-grid** (6): `2 rows of 2 (or 3) equal rectangles, equal row heights.`

**Tall panels:** the tall panel is always first or last in reading order, never in the middle. **Diagonals** (rare): slant only the dividing line between two neighbouring panels in the same row, about 10 degrees from vertical, the same angle and gutter everywhere on the canvas, never touching the frame or running between rows; every panel keeps a flat horizontal top and bottom edge and its label sits centered under that flat edge; allowed variants are Diagonal pair (2), Diagonal triple (3) and Diagonal splash + strip (4 or 5), only for action, conflict or sudden reversal.

## 5. RULES THAT ALWAYS APPLY (paste as the bullet list under the geometry phrase)
**Layout 1:**
- Panels sit in horizontal rows. Every panel in one row has the same top and bottom edge (a circle's diameter equals the row height), so each row is a clean horizontal band. Rows never overlap each other vertically and the rows together fill the frame height{ below the ribbon}. Rows may hold different numbers of panels and are centered.
- The story is read left to right inside a row and rows top to bottom, in exactly the panel order of PANEL CONTENTS. There are no right-to-left rows, no rings and no panels arranged in columns that break the row bands.
- All panels of the same shape and role are the same size. Even gutters of empty white space separate every panel. No panel touches, overlaps or crosses another panel or the frame, and every row leaves room for the labels attached under its panels.

**Layout 2:**
- Panels sit in horizontal rows. Every panel in one row has the same top and bottom edge, so each row is a clean horizontal band. Rows never overlap vertically and no panel spans two rows (Tall layouts excepted). The rows together fill the frame height{ below the ribbon} and every row leaves room for the labels attached under its panels.
- Panels in one row share the row height and together fill the row width, with widths following the percentages in the LAYOUT line. All rows have the same total width and are centered.
- All panels are rectangles with square corners. The biggest panel named in the LAYOUT line is clearly the largest by area.
- Every gutter on the canvas has the same width (a gutter that holds the label of the panel above it may be taller).
- The story is read left to right inside a row and rows top to bottom, in exactly the panel order of PANEL CONTENTS. There are no slanted edges, no insets, no overlapping or borderless panels, no right-to-left rows and no rings.

## 6. GUTTERS
One setting per canvas, written in the LAYOUT line: **tight**, **standard** (default), **wide** (calm, reflective, single panels, closing lines).
