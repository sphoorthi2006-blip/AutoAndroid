# PACK: HISTORY (cel-shaded, with modern-story rules)

History, empires, wars, ancient and medieval worlds, inventions, companies and economies, plus story-driven videos set in the recent past or present (true crime, mysteries, technology and AI stories, business stories, modern science told through people). Applies on top of `rules.md`, `layouts.md` and `template.md`. STYLE uses the HISTORY alternatives. Explainer videos (a concept or mechanism, not a story) belong to the Health and Finance skill.

## THE ERA BRACKET
Fill `[ERA, YEAR, REGION AND CULTURE]` in STYLE for every canvas (for example "Mauryan Pataliputra, 3rd century BCE"). Modern: `present day, [region]`, or `[decade or year], [region]` for the recent past. If a modern script names no region, use one generic modern city and keep it for the whole file. A panel that jumps to another era names it in its own description.

## SCENE PANEL KINDS
Person line (a leader, soldier, merchant, scientist, explorer or ordinary person doing something); event or action (the moment through the people acting and the objects that prove it; battles stylized, never gory); place or architecture (the place large and whole with a little life if implied); **map panel** (aged parchment-style map in the era's visual language with warm worn parchment texture, flat color regions, clear coastlines, borders and routes, and only the regions, cities or routes the cue names, up to 4 hand-inked names; **route or movement arrows are allowed on a map panel only**); object, artifact or invention (large and whole with a hint of use); company or organization (through its buildings, ships, products, workers, offices; never a real logo); concept (one concrete scene that proves it: a crowded port for trade, a tax collector counting grain for taxation); comparison (two matching drawings in one panel separated by a natural element); time passing (the same place changing, ruins growing, a character visibly older, season or light change); modern or present-day line; list line (up to 4 items, each its own clear object); **heading text canvas** (see below); **like-and-subscribe canvas** (`TEXT-CANVAS` in the plan: a plain white text slide made with TEMPLATE B in `template.md`, the words LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE in black Patrick Hand lettering, no icon, box, color or label).
- **Heading canvas** (`TEXT-CANVAS` in the plan): NOT a comic canvas and not a colored sheet. It is a plain text slide made with TEMPLATE B in `template.md`: pure white screen, the heading's exact words in black Patrick Hand lettering, nothing else (no frame, box, color or label).
- There are no verse panels in this pack unless the script quotes a document or source at length; a quoted source is drawn as the scene or as a document panel (a parchment or paper document with the quote words in 1-3 lines, only if the cue is that quote).

## CHARACTERS (no fixed cast; every one is created from the script)
Lock every character before writing: age range, gender, skin tone, hair color, length and style, beard or moustache, build, a distinctive facial feature, exact clothing (colors, fabric, headwear, footwear, sash or belt), one or two identifying accessories (crown, staff, sword, ring, medal, satchel), all correct for era, region, culture and rank. Real historical figures are described by look (age, build, dress, rank, signature items), never by name; the name appears only as text on a natural carrier and only if the cue needs it. Roles are shown through costume, action and one key prop. Background crowds, armies and bystanders have simple readable faces and period-correct dress and never compete with the main subject. Every character shows a real feeling and an active pose (ruling, trading, marching, building, praying, negotiating, writing).

## TEXT RULES
1. TEXT TEST: add text only if the viewer would misunderstand the narration without it. Most panels need none.
2. IN-WORLD CARRIERS ONLY. Ancient and medieval: stone inscription, banner, scroll or parchment, document, coin, shop or city signboard, ship's flag, map caption. Modern: phone, laptop or monitor screen (at most one short word or number, never a full interface, chat or code), shop or street sign, office door plate, document or contract, report page, newspaper, ID badge, whiteboard or wall notes in an office or classroom, product packaging with a generic invented name, dashboard or meter, banner. Never floating text, captions, subtitles or hovering labels.
3. HOW MUCH: 0 to 3 pieces of text per panel, each 1 to 4 words, in the script's own words and spelling. Numbers and dates the viewer needs appear as numerals on an era-correct carrier (carved stone, banner, map caption, document, signboard); ancient and medieval scenes never use clocks, printed calendars or modern fonts. Each number is shown once. Idioms and vague amounts get no number.
4. In a panel that has text, list the exact text with its carrier and position in the panel description and say it is the only text in that panel. The canvas SPACING AND TEXT line then uses the "text panels" alternative.
5. Text sits on its carrier in clean space, never covering a face or overlapping other text. Quantities keep one scale per panel (twice as many ships is about twice as many ships).

## INVISIBLE IDEAS
Power or rule: a ruler on a throne or dais with officials bowing, guards, seals, banners. Wealth or trade: loaded ships, full storehouses, merchants counting coins, crowded markets. Decline or defeat: cracked walls, empty markets, abandoned fields, a lowered flag, tired soldiers. Influence or spread: the same object or practice appearing in several places or hands. Rivalry or conflict: two sides facing each other with one object between them. Time passing: visible change in the same place or the same character. Modern ideas: trust = two people and a held-out key or document; risk = a person at the edge of a decision with the visible cost in the foreground; growth or decline = the same place across matching drawings; attention = a gaze fixed on one object while the room goes on; habit = the same person, place and action repeated. Never icons, arrows or glow.

## MODERN SCENE RULES
1. NO REAL BRANDS: no real logos, brand names, product designs or app interfaces; devices, vehicles, products and apps are generic and unbranded; a named company appears only as plain signage or document text if the cue needs it.
2. TECHNOLOGY AND AI: show the idea through people using, building, testing or reacting to ordinary devices, server rooms, factory lines, labs, cables and screens with one short word. Never glowing brains, floating circuits, holograms, neon data streams, film robots or abstract blue light. An algorithm or data is a person at a screen plus the visible result.
3. BUSINESS: offices, shops, warehouses, factories, meetings, products, delivery vehicles, boxes and receipts; a number is a numeral on a screen, document or sign.
4. SCIENCE: laboratories, field sites, observatories, specimens, instruments, with people doing the work; a discovery is the moment of the person seeing the result.
5. PSYCHOLOGY: a person in a believable situation whose posture, face, hands and surroundings prove the idea; thoughts and feelings are never glowing symbols, thought bubbles or brain icons.
6. Interview, call or message: two people facing each other, or one person holding a phone with their reaction visible; message text is never written. Courtroom or meeting: the room, the people in their places, one document; no names as text.

## TRUE CRIME SAFETY (highest priority for that niche)
No gore, blood, wounds, bodies, graphic injuries or detailed weapons; show the situation by what comes before or after (a dark street, an empty room, a closed door, police tape from a distance, evidence bags, a documents table, a worried face, a courtroom). A victim is shown with respect, never injured or in a harmful pose; violence is implied by aftermath, never depicted. Real people, victims and suspects are stylized and described by look, never by name as text; never draw text that accuses a named real person; add no guilt, motive or detail the cue does not state. Evidence panels (a wall of papers, a map with markers) are allowed with 0 to 3 pieces of 1-4 word text. Children are shown safely and calmly, never in a harmful or frightening situation.

## SAFE IMAGERY
War, violence and death are stylized, never graphic: show battles through formations, banners, dust, clashing shields, retreat and aftermath. No nudity. Slavery, massacres and dark topics through the situation and the emotion (chains seen from a distance, grieving faces, a burning town far in the background), never explicit cruelty. Where history calls for gender-segregated spaces or roles, reflect it accurately and show women in the roles and areas historically correct for them. No real brand logos, trademarks or watermarks. No decorative filler or random objects.

## ERA AND ACCURACY
Every panel belongs fully to the era, region and culture of its cue: architecture, materials, clothing, hairstyles, weapons, tools, vehicles and ships, food, containers, writing surfaces, flags and symbols. Anachronism check: never an object, garment, building style, weapon, flag or technology that did not exist at that time and place.

## PACK SELF-CHECK
The era bracket is filled and nothing is anachronistic; text only on natural carriers, 0-3 pieces of 1-4 words; arrows only on map panels; no gore, nudity or explicit cruelty; no real brands; true-crime and modern rules held; no circles, ribbon or soft glow anywhere.
