# LAYOUTS - Bible and History (comic layouts only)

Bible and History tell what happened, so every canvas is a **comic layout**: rectangles of different sizes sitting directly on the white canvas with NO outer frame or border around them, the key beat in the biggest panel, flow carried by order, size and caption boxes. **No arrows, no lines, no connectors, no circles, no ribbon** on these canvases.

## 1. HOW TO CHOOSE (do this silently for every canvas)
1. **Name the story shape** of the paragraph from its cues: hook then details, build-up to a payoff, turning point (before / turn / after), journey or time passing, contrast or rivalry, cause then result, action or conflict, quiet or reflective moment, one big idea, a quoted passage (verse).
2. **Count the beats** = the panel count from the canvas plan. The layout MUST have exactly that many panels. Never pad, never trim.
3. **Find the key beat** (the strongest or most turning cue) and put it in the biggest panel, or the widest band for a verse. If no cue is clearly strongest, use a layout with equal panels.
4. **Set the mood:** calm or reflective = standard or wide gutter, straight cuts. Fast, tense or energetic (battle, chase, shock, collapse) = tight gutter, and a diagonal layout only when the beat truly is action, conflict or a sudden reversal.
5. **Check rhythm** against the previous canvases (the LAYOUT lines of the prompts already written, and the layout log in the locks):
   - never the same layout twice in a row;
   - never more than two canvases in a row from the same family (Bands, Tall, Diagonal, Grid);
   - diagonals no more than about one canvas in three, and only for action, conflict or shock;
   - grids (Four-grid, Six-grid) only for truly parallel items, and rarely (about one canvas in eight at most), so other layouts land harder;
   - after a busy 5 to 8 panel canvas, a single-panel canvas is a welcome pause.
6. **Verse paragraphs:** see section 4.
7. **A single-cue paragraph is a pause or a punch:** use Single splash. A short hook, question or closing line gets a wide gutter and one clear, simple picture; an event or scene gets the full cinematic scene.
8. Parts of one split paragraph are separate canvases: give each its own layout of the same mood but never the same layout.

## 2. THE LIBRARY (pick by panel count; widths are shares of the row width, heights are shares of the canvas height)

| Panels | Layout | Family | Key beat | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single splash | Bands | the only panel | lone line, hook, pause, closing line |
| 2 | Pair | Bands | the wider panel | before and after, rivals, question and answer, claim and result |
| 2 | Diagonal pair | Diagonal | the wider panel | sudden conflict, clash, shock |
| 3 | Widescreen strips | Bands | the tallest band | journey, passing time, quiet moments |
| 3 | Tall-left / Tall-right | Tall | the tall panel | one hero beat with two supporting beats |
| 3 | Diagonal triple | Diagonal | the widest panel | action in three beats |
| 4 | Splash + strip | Bands | first | hook or outcome first, then its parts |
| 4 | Strip + splash | Bands | last | build-up to a payoff |
| 4 | Widescreen strips (4 bands) | Bands | the tallest band | slow journey |
| 4 | Stagger | Bands | the widest panel | back-and-forth, cause and result |
| 4 | Four-grid | Grid | none | four parallel items |
| 5 | Turning point | Bands | panel 3 | before, turn, after |
| 5 | Sandwich | Bands | panel 3 or 4 (middle of the middle row) | setup, main moment, aftermath |
| 5 | Splash + strip (5) / Strip + splash (5) | Bands | first / last | as above with four small panels |
| 5 | Tall-left with bottom row | Tall | panel 1 | hero beat, supports, then two closing beats |
| 5 | Diagonal splash + strip | Diagonal | first | action hook, then its parts |
| 6 | Crescendo | Bands | last | rising tension |
| 6 | Reveal-down | Bands | first | big claim, then consequences |
| 6 | Stagger (6) | Bands | the widest panel | back-and-forth sequence |

| 7 | Centerpiece | Bands | panel 4 | long build to a middle peak, then aftermath |
| 8 | Hourglass | Bands | panels 4 and 5 | long section with a middle peak |

Panels are **rectangles with square corners**. The only exception is the Diagonal family, where the dividing line between two neighbouring panels in the same row is slanted (see section 3). Panel outlines, gutters, labels and caption boxes are flat and bold; there is no outer frame.

### Geometry phrases (paste into the LAYOUT line, filling the gutter setting; rows are listed top to bottom, panels left to right)
- **Single splash** (1): `1 row. One wide rectangle, panel 1, about 80 percent of the canvas width and filling the canvas height, centered.`
- **Pair** (2): `1 row. Two rectangles, panel 1 about 60 percent and panel 2 about 40 percent of the row width, the row filling the canvas height.` (the wider one holds the key beat; flip 40/60 if the key beat is second; 50/50 only when equal)
- **Widescreen strips** (3 or 4): `3 rows, each row one full-width rectangle: panel 1 (short), panel 2 (medium), panel 3 (tall, the largest by area); the row heights are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4.` (4 rows: ratio 2 : 2 : 3 : 4, or put the tallest band where the key beat is: say which)
- **Tall-left** (3): `Left column: one tall rectangle, panel 1, about 40 percent of the width and the full height, the largest panel. Right column (about 60 percent): two stacked rectangles, panel 2 on top and panel 3 below, each half the height, with a gutter between them tall enough to hold panel 2's label.` **Tall-right** (3): mirror it, and the tall panel is panel 3 (read the stack first, top to bottom, then the tall panel). The tall panel is always FIRST or LAST in reading order, never in the middle.
- **Tall-left with bottom row** (5): the Tall-left block fills the top band (about 62 percent of the height); the bottom band (about 38 percent) holds two rectangles, panels 4 and 5, 50 / 50.
- **Splash + strip** (4 or 5): `2 rows. Row 1: one wide rectangle, panel 1, clearly the largest panel (height ratio 3). Row 2: three (or four) equal rectangles, panels 2 to 4 (or 5) (height ratio 2).`
- **Strip + splash** (4 or 5): the same, with the row of small panels first and the wide panel last (the last panel is the largest).
- **Turning point** (5): `3 rows. Row 1: two rectangles, 50 / 50, panels 1 and 2. Row 2: one wide rectangle, panel 3, clearly the largest panel. Row 3: two rectangles, 50 / 50, panels 4 and 5. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Sandwich** (5): `3 rows. Row 1: one wide rectangle, panel 1. Row 2: three rectangles, panel 2 (about 25 percent), panel 3 (about 50 percent, the largest), panel 4 (about 25 percent). Row 3: one wide rectangle, panel 5. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Crescendo** (6): `3 rows. Row 1: three equal rectangles, panels 1 to 3. Row 2: two rectangles, 50 / 50, panels 4 and 5. Row 3: one wide rectangle, panel 6, clearly the largest. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 4.`
- **Reveal-down** (6): `3 rows. Row 1: one wide rectangle, panel 1, clearly the largest. Row 2: two rectangles, 50 / 50, panels 2 and 3. Row 3: three equal rectangles, panels 4 to 6. Height ratio 4 : 3 : 2.`
- **Stagger** (4 or 6): `2 (or 3) rows of two rectangles. Row 1: panel 1 about 60 percent, panel 2 about 40 percent. Row 2: panel 3 about 40 percent, panel 4 about 60 percent. (Row 3: panel 5 about 60 percent, panel 6 about 40 percent.) Equal row heights.`
- **Centerpiece** (7): `3 rows. Row 1: three equal rectangles, panels 1 to 3. Row 2: one wide rectangle, panel 4, clearly the largest. Row 3: three equal rectangles, panels 5 to 7. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Hourglass** (8): `3 rows. Row 1: three equal rectangles, panels 1 to 3. Row 2: two wide rectangles, panels 4 and 5 (about 50 percent each), the largest panels. Row 3: three equal rectangles, panels 6 to 8. Height ratio 2 : 3 : 2.`
- **Four-grid** (4): `2 rows of 2 equal rectangles, equal row heights.` **Six-grid** (6): `2 rows of 3 equal rectangles, equal row heights.` All panels equal; no panel is the key.

## 3. DIAGONAL FAMILY (occasional; action, conflict, shock, sudden reversal only)
Slant only the dividing line **between two neighbouring panels in the same row**. Every panel keeps a flat horizontal top and bottom edge, so each row stays a clean horizontal band and the reading order is unchanged (left to right, rows top to bottom). All slanted dividers on one canvas use the same slight angle (about 10 degrees from vertical) and the same gutter width. A slanted divider never touches the image edge and never runs between rows. Panels become parallelograms: each title label is centered under its panel's flat bottom edge, and nothing important sits in the pointed corners.
- **Diagonal pair** (2): Pair with one slanted divider.
- **Diagonal triple** (3): one row, three panels (about 30 / 40 / 30), two slanted dividers, the widest in the middle or the side of the key beat.
- **Diagonal splash + strip** (4 or 5): Splash + strip whose bottom row has slanted dividers; the wide top panel stays square-cornered and straight.

## 4. VERSES (parchment text panels)
A cue flagged `VERSE` in the canvas plan is a **parchment text panel**: a flat cream parchment sheet that fills the panel and carries the cue's exact words (see the pack). It is the one kind of panel that holds text.
- A verse run (consecutive verse cues) sits together in the **widest row(s)** of the layout, so the lettering is large. A paragraph that is all verse uses **Verse strips**: `N rows, each row one full-width rectangle (equal heights), panels 1 to N top to bottom, every panel a parchment text panel.` (N up to 8; wider gutter; no key beat)
- A mixed paragraph (narration, then the quote, then narration) uses a normal layout whose widest band holds the verse panels: for example Splash + strip with a one-cue verse in the wide band, Strip + splash with the verse last, Turning point with a one-cue verse in the middle, Widescreen strips with verse bands. Choose by where the verse falls in the cue order, because the order must stay the cue order.
- Never mix parchment text and scene art inside one panel. Every parchment panel on a video uses the identical cream parchment, ink color, lettering style and layout; only the words change.
- Never put a verse in a Diagonal or Grid layout.

## 5. GUTTERS
One gutter setting per canvas, written in the LAYOUT line: **tight** (fast, energetic), **standard** (default), **wide** (calm, reflective, verses, single splash). Every gutter on the canvas has the same width, except a gutter that must be taller to hold the label of the panel above it.

## 6. LAYOUT RULES THAT ALWAYS APPLY (paste as the bullet list under the geometry phrase)
- Panels sit in horizontal rows. Every panel in one row has the same top and bottom edge, so each row is a clean horizontal band. Rows never overlap vertically and no panel spans two rows (except the Tall layouts as described). The rows together fill the canvas height and every row leaves room for the labels attached under its panels.
- Panels in one row share the row height and together fill the row width, with widths following the percentages in the LAYOUT line. All rows have the same total width and are centered.
- All panels are rectangles with square corners [Diagonal family: except the slanted dividers described above]. The biggest panel named in the LAYOUT line is clearly the largest by area.
- Every gutter on the canvas has the same width.
- The story is read left to right inside a row and rows top to bottom, in exactly the panel order of PANEL CONTENTS. There are no slanted edges (except as described), no insets, no overlapping or borderless panels, no right-to-left rows and no rings.
